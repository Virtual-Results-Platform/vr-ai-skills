# Exceptional blogging

The bar: a reader who knows the area should think "this person actually lives
here," and a reader who doesn't should finish the post anyway.

## Before writing
- Name the ONE reader. "Buyers" is nobody. "A family relocating to Ashburn this
  summer with a $700k budget" writes half the post for you.
- Name the ONE job the post does for them (answer a question, calm a fear, surface
  homes they'd miss). Everything that doesn't serve that job gets cut.
- Find the one concrete fact competitors' posts won't have: a price change on a
  real street, days-on-market shift, what $450k bought here last year vs now.
  Listing data from search_listings is your unfair advantage — use real numbers.

## Openers (first 2 sentences decide everything)
- Open with the reader's situation or a concrete scene — never with the topic.
  Bad: "The Atlanta real estate market has seen many changes in 2026."
  Good: "The townhome at 2106 Garden Place got 11 showings its first weekend."
- No throat-clearing: never "In today's fast-paced market", "Whether you're a
  first-time buyer or seasoned investor", "Grab your coffee".
- The first sentence may not contain the words "market", "landscape", or "journey".

## Body craft
- One idea per paragraph, 1-3 sentences each. Blog readers scan; walls lose them.
- Specific beats general, always: "$12k over asking" beats "competitive offers";
  "the BeltLine's eastside trail" beats "great amenities nearby".
- Verbs carry sentences. Cut adverbs, cut "very/truly/really", cut hedges
  ("can be", "may find", "tends to") unless the uncertainty is the point.
- Numbers get context or they're noise: "$450,000 — about $40k under the intown
  townhome median" not just "$450,000".
- Write like you talk to a client at a kitchen table. Contractions yes. Jargon
  translated ("Coming Soon — meaning you can tour it before it hits the portals").
- Every listing mention earns its place with a WHY-care line, not a spec dump —
  specs are one line, the hook is human ("the top floor is a guest suite with its
  own balcony over the trees").

## Banned (AI-slop tells — auto-reject on sight)
- "nestled", "boasts", "stunning", "breathtaking", "hidden gem", "oasis",
  "seamlessly", "elevate", "unlock", "dive in", "look no further", "dream home",
  "in the heart of", "whether you're X or Y", "it's important to note",
  "in conclusion", "game-changer", "vibrant community" (name what's actually there
  instead), em-dash chains, exclamation points in body copy.
- Rule of thumb: if the sentence could appear on any brokerage's blog about any
  city, delete it and write the version only THIS site could publish.

## Structure & SEO (works with listing-blog-posts field mechanics)
- H2s are scannable claims or questions a local would search, not labels:
  "What $450k buys in East Atlanta right now" beats "Atlanta Listings".
- Primary keyword: title, first 100 words, one H2, meta description — then stop.
  Density chasing reads robotic and Google knows it.
- Local SEO gold is specificity: neighborhood names, school names, commute
  anchors, the actual highway exits. That's what ranks and converts (small-volume
  local terms beat big-money generic terms for these sites).
- Internal links: every post links at least one listing page and one relevant
  site page (neighborhood guide, agent page) with descriptive anchor text —
  never "click here".
- Length: as long as the substance, not a word longer. 600 strong words beat
  1400 padded ones. No padding to hit a count.

## Close
- End with ONE specific next step tied to the post's content ("Want to tour 2106
  Garden Place before Saturday's open house? Call/text ___"). Not three CTAs,
  not "reach out today to learn more".

## Final pass (do this every time, before saving the draft)
1. Read the first sentence alone — would a local keep reading?
2. Sweep for every banned phrase; rewrite, don't thesaurus-swap.
3. Verify every number/claim against the listing data actually returned — no
   remembered stats, no invented market claims.
4. Cut 10%. There's always 10%.
